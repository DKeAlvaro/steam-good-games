Do great games get ignored on Steam?

I had an AI rate the store page of 14,836 Steam games: the art, the screenshots, the description. Then I checked which games actually found players.

The fine print

How I did it
  • The list: 85,152 Steam games with their review counts (SteamSpy).
  • The sample: 14,836 games, picked so that every level of success is covered, from 0 reviews to millions. Each game is weighted so results represent all of Steam.
  • The page: capsule image, 4 screenshots and the descriptions, from Steam's store API.
  • The judge: an AI (Jev by TypeSafe) answered 46 questions per page. The page score is the average of 29 quality ratings. It never saw review counts.
  • Testing the judge: asked twice, it gave the same answers (r = 0.99). A human rated 240 pages blind, and the AI agreed on which page was better 7–8 times out of 10.
  • Success: Steam hides sales, so I used reviews. Rule of thumb: 1 review ≈ 30–60 copies.
  • The whole AI run took 12 minutes and cost about $4.
What this doesn't prove
  • Linked isn't caused. Teams with great pages often also have money and fans. Facts like who made it, price and genre predict 65% of the differences between games. Adding page quality gets to 69%, and page quality alone gets 45%.
  • Pages are today's. Hits often polish their page later. Keeping only games whose art wasn't updated a year after launch weakens the link, but it stays.
  • The AI knows famous games like Hades. Removing every game it recognized weakens the link a bit, but it stays.
  • It rates the page, not the fun.
  • Brand-new games are under-counted in the list, so the real "ignored" share is probably higher, mostly for weak pages.
Numbers for nerds
